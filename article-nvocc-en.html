What Is an NVOCC (Non-Vessel Operating Common Carrier)?

Ocean Freight · 3 min read

An NVOCC is an ocean carrier that transports goods under its own House Bill of Lading — or equivalent documentation — without operating ocean-going vessels.

How Does an NVOCC Work?

  • It leases vessel space from a VOCC (Vessel Operating Common Carrier) and sells that space to its own customers.
  • An NVOCC can be described as a shipper to carriers and a carrier to shippers — a dual role.
  • NVOCCs usually do not own warehouses, but many own their own fleet of containers.
  • In certain circumstances, an NVOCC may also operate as a freight forwarder.

VOCC vs. NVOCC

  • VOCC: owns and operates vessels, issues the Master Bill of Lading.
  • NVOCC: buys capacity through volume contracts or space chartering and issues its own House B/L to shippers.
  • On the Master B/L, the NVOCC typically appears as the shipper.

How Is It Different from a Freight Forwarder?

  • A traditional forwarder acts as an agent; an NVOCC acts as the carrier and takes responsibility for carriage.
  • Many companies hold both roles and can serve customers under either capacity.
  • LCL consolidations are typically run by NVOCCs: multiple shipments share one container, and each shipper receives its own House B/L.
Why it matters: NVOCCs secure space and rates through volume commitments — often stronger protection in peak season. Verify your partner's NVOCC qualification when booking.
