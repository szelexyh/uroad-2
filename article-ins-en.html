Cargo Insurance vs. Cargo Liability Insurance

Insurance · 4 min read

Cargo insurance and cargo liability insurance both come into play when cargo is lost or damaged in transit — but they protect two very different parties. Knowing the difference tells you exactly who pays when something goes wrong.

Cargo Insurance — Protects You, the Cargo Owner

  • Covers the policyholder's (e.g., the owner of the goods) investment in the goods.
  • Provides financial protection when insured goods are lost or damaged during transportation.
  • Issued by an insurance company directly to the cargo owner.
  • Pays for the damage regardless of the fault of the party transporting the cargo.
  • Common forms: all-risk cargo insurance and named-perils cargo insurance.

Cargo Liability Insurance — Protects the Transportation Company

  • Covers the carrier's responsibility to compensate you for loss or damage.
  • Pays only when the transportation company is held liable for the loss.
  • Common forms: motor carrier cargo insurance and ocean cargo liability insurance.
  • Inherently limited: the carrier may invoke legal defenses to avoid or cap liability.

The Key Difference in One Sentence

Cargo insurance protects you against your financial losses; cargo liability insurance protects the transportation company from the financial impact of your claims.

Practical advice: never rely on a carrier's liability coverage alone — compensation is capped and fault must be proven. For valuable or sensitive cargo, purchase cargo insurance and confirm coverage terms before shipment.
