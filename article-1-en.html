The 9 Classes of Dangerous Goods Explained

U ROAD Logistics · Updated Jan 2025 · 3 min read

In international transport, dangerous goods (DG) are classified into nine classes under the International Maritime Dangerous Goods (IMDG) Code. Each class carries strict compliance requirements in booking, declaration, packaging and container loading. Here is a quick overview of the nine classes and typical products in each.

Class 1: Explosives

Fireworks, detonators, ammunition, etc. Most ocean carriers decline this class; it requires special approval and dedicated channels.

Class 2: Gases

Compressed gases, aerosols (spray cans), lighters, fire extinguishers, etc. Subdivided into flammable, non-flammable non-toxic, and toxic gases.

Class 3: Flammable Liquids

Paint, coatings, alcohol, perfume, nail polish, adhesives, etc. One of the most common classes in daily exports.

Class 4: Flammable Solids; Substances Liable to Spontaneous Combustion; Substances Emitting Flammable Gases in Contact with Water

Sulfur, matches, metallic sodium, certain activated carbon, etc.

Class 5: Oxidizing Substances and Organic Peroxides

Hydrogen peroxide, potassium permanganate, bleaching powder, etc. Risk of decomposition and explosion under heat or impact.

Class 6: Toxic and Infectious Substances

Pesticides, certain chemical raw materials, medical waste, etc.

Class 7: Radioactive Material

Certain testing instruments and isotope products; requires specially licensed carriers.

Class 8: Corrosives

Batteries containing acid, hydrochloric acid, caustic soda, cleaning agents, etc.

Class 9: Miscellaneous Dangerous Substances and Articles

The broadest class — and the one most relevant to cross-border e-commerce and the energy storage industry. Lithium batteries and products containing lithium batteries (UN3480 lithium-ion batteries, UN3481 lithium batteries contained in equipment), e-bikes, hoverboards, portable power stations and automotive airbags all fall into this class.

What Do You Need to Book Dangerous Goods?

  • MSDS (Safety Data Sheet) in English
  • Export dangerous goods packaging certificate (危包证)
  • Accurate product name, UN number and packaging photos
  • The carrier's DG booking approval (policies vary by carrier)

DG exports involve many steps and strict compliance — a single mismatch can lead to booking rejection or even penalties.
