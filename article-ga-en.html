What Is General Average? A Costly Risk for Uninsured Cargo

Maritime Risk · 4 min read

General Average (GA) is a maritime legal concept: when a vessel and its cargo face a common peril, the costs of saving them are shared among all parties with an interest in the voyage, in proportion to value — even if your own cargo was never damaged.

When Is GA Declared?

  • A vessel is stranded, disabled or suffers engine failure.
  • Cargo is jettisoned to save the vessel.
  • Cargo is damaged in order to protect the vessel (e.g., water used to fight a fire).

How Is the Contribution Calculated?

A GA adjuster conducts the adjustment, considering the value of the vessel, the value of the cargo and the value of any other interests involved in the voyage — each party then contributes in proportion.

Does Insurance Cover GA?

  • GA is governed by international maritime law and is a complex legal concept.
  • Standard cargo insurance does not necessarily cover GA contributions — coverage depends on the policy terms; always confirm before shipment.
  • If your policy excludes GA, consider purchasing a separate GA guarantee or bond to protect against this risk.
Risk alert: once GA is declared, uninsured cargo owners may be required to post a security deposit before cargo can be released. For valuable shipments, confirm your policy covers GA contributions before sailing.
