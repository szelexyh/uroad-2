What Is LCL (Less than Container Load)?

Ocean Freight · 3 min read

LCL is a mode of shipping via ocean, recommended if your shipment isn't large enough to fill a container. Your cargo is consolidated with other LCL shipments into one container — you only pay for the space you use.

How an LCL Shipment Moves

  • Consolidation at origin: cargo is delivered to a CFS (Container Freight Station), consolidated with other shippers' cargo into one container, then gated in at the CY (Container Yard).
  • Ocean transit: the container carries cargo for multiple consignees on the same vessel.
  • Deconsolidation at destination: the container is stripped at the destination CFS, and each consignee picks up their own cargo.

LCL or FCL — Which Fits Your Shipment?

  • Charging: LCL is billed by W/M — per CBM or per 1,000 kg, whichever is greater. Below roughly 15 CBM, LCL usually costs less; close to a full 20' load, FCL is often cheaper per unit.
  • Transit time: consolidation and deconsolidation add a few days versus FCL on the same route.
  • Handling: LCL cargo is stuffed and stripped at CFS, so reinforce packing and palletize where possible.
  • Destination charges: per-CBM fees at destination are relatively higher for LCL — compare the total cost, not just the origin-side rate.
Quick rule: small-to-medium shipments that can't fill a box → LCL; near-full-container volumes, or high-value cargo you'd rather not mix with others → FCL.
