What Is an MSDS and When Do You Need One?

Documents · 4 min read

An MSDS (Material Safety Data Sheet) documents the potential hazards of a product and how to handle it safely. It is required for all dangerous — or even just potentially dangerous — shipments, and the supplier is responsible for providing all hazmat documentation.

Which Shipments Need an MSDS?

  • All potentially dangerous products: liquids, creams and powders may need an MSDS simply to prove they are not dangerous.
  • All lithium battery shipments — whether classified as dangerous or not.
  • Dry and alkaline batteries: carriers may also request an MSDS to certify they are not lithium-based.
  • Forwarders and carriers require an MSDS copy before shipment; missing documents can hold up booking and uplift.

How Do I Know If an MSDS Is Expired?

Go to Section 14, "Transport Information", and check which edition of the IATA DGR (Dangerous Goods Regulations) the product was packed under:

  • MSDS layouts differ by supplier, but every MSDS is divided into numbered sections.
  • IATA releases the next year's DGR edition every October, effective January 1st.
  • An MSDS referencing an outdated edition is expired — ask your supplier for an updated copy.

Can I Re-use an MSDS?

  • Only if the hazardous material is exactly the same: the same lithium battery used across different products can share one MSDS.
  • The MSDS must still be provided for every shipment to certify the hazardous material has not changed.
Responsibility: properly packaging, labeling and documenting potentially dangerous cargo is the shipper's sole responsibility. Confirm with your supplier that the MSDS is complete and current before shipping.
